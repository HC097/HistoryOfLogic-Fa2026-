# History of Logic — Lecture Notes

This repository contains my lecture notes for a course on the **History of Logic**, following the historical development of logical thought from its Greek beginnings through later formal and mathematical logic.

The notes are written primarily as **university lecture materials**, rather than as textbook transcriptions. They are designed to accompany recorded lectures and include the definitions, arguments, examples, derivations, and whiteboard material used during instruction.

## Primary Source

The principal historical source for the course is:

> William Kneale and Martha Kneale,  
> *The Development of Logic*.  
> Oxford: Clarendon Press.

The lectures generally follow the historical progression of the Kneales' work while reorganizing material where useful for teaching.

The source is treated as the authoritative basis for the historical presentation of the course. The notes aim to preserve its terminology, distinctions, examples, and historical framing while presenting the material in a form suitable for lectures.

---

## Lecture Philosophy

The central rule governing these notes is:

> **IF WE SPEAK, WE WRITE.**

Anything substantive discussed during a lecture should also have a written counterpart that students can record.

This includes:

- definitions,
- logical principles,
- equations,
- argument forms,
- distinctions,
- examples,
- derivations,
- diagrams,
- historical relationships,
- and major conclusions.

There should be no important invisible layer of information that exists only in the spoken lecture.
